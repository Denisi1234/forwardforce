Apparel

DTF printing vs embroidery: which is right for your brand?

When branding T-shirts, polos, hoodies or caps, the two most popular methods we use are DTF (direct-to-film) printing and embroidery. Each has clear strengths.

Choose DTF printing when...

  • Your design has many colours, gradients or photographic detail.
  • You need large prints, such as a full back design.
  • You want a soft, flexible finish on cotton, polyester or blends.
  • You need small quantities or several different designs.

Choose embroidery when...

  • You want a premium, professional look for corporate uniforms, polos and caps.
  • Durability matters: embroidery survives frequent washing for years.
  • Your logo is relatively simple, with bold shapes and limited fine text.

Our tip

Many clients combine both: an embroidered logo on the chest for a professional look and a DTF print on the back for event or campaign messaging. Talk to us and we will recommend the best option for your garment and budget.

Artwork

How to prepare print-ready artwork in 6 steps

The most common cause of printing delays is artwork that is not ready for print. Follow these steps before you send your file.

  • Set the correct size and add 3 mm bleed on every side.
  • Keep important content 3-5 mm inside the trim line.
  • Use 300 dpi images at the final print size.
  • Convert to CMYK to avoid colour shifts between screen and print.
  • Outline or embed fonts so text prints exactly as designed.
  • Export as PDF (or send AI/EPS/SVG) with crop marks if possible.

See the full size chart on our templates page, or request a template file for your product. Not sure? Send us your file and we will check it before printing.

Corporate Branding

Planning branded staff uniforms: a simple checklist

Well-branded uniforms make your team easy to recognise and present a consistent image to customers. Before you order, work through this checklist.

  • Garment type: polos for a smart-casual look, T-shirts for events and field teams, jackets or hoodies for cooler conditions.
  • Fabric: breathable cotton or cotton blends work well in Dar es Salaam's climate.
  • Branding method: embroidery for a long-lasting premium finish, DTF for colourful or large designs.
  • Logo placement: left chest is standard; add sleeves or back for extra visibility.
  • Sizes: collect a size list from your team early to avoid shortages.
  • Timeline: allow time for a proof, approval and production, especially before launches or events.

Ready to start? ask about fabric and embroidery samples or ask for a quote.

Signs & Banners

Choosing the right banner for your event

The right banner gets your brand seen; the wrong one gets ignored. Here is a quick guide.

Roll-up banners

Portable and quick to set up, roll-ups are ideal for conferences, exhibitions, receptions and indoor events. A standard size is 85 x 200 cm.

Vinyl (PVC) banners

Weather-resistant and available in custom sizes, vinyl banners with eyelets are best for outdoor venues, shop fronts, fences and stages.

Teardrop and feather flags

Tall, eye-catching and visible from a distance, flags are excellent for roadside promotions, launches and outdoor activations.

Design tips

  • Keep the message short: a logo, one headline and a contact detail.
  • Use large, bold text that can be read from several metres away.
  • Use high contrast between text and background.

Marketing Materials

Make your business cards stand out

A business card is often the first physical item a client receives from you. Small upgrades make a big difference.

  • Thicker stock: a heavier card (around 350gsm) feels more professional.
  • Matte or gloss: matte looks elegant and is easy to write on; gloss makes colours pop.
  • Soft-touch lamination: a velvety finish that people remember.
  • Foil accents: gold or silver foil on your logo adds a premium touch.
  • Rounded corners or square cards: a different shape helps you stand out from the pile.
